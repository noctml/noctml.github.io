Context: dynamic object와 monocular ambiguity가 왜 어려운가

Introduction은 기존 VO/SLAM이 정적 환경 가정에 묶여 있고, moving object가 data association과 map quality를 무너뜨린다는 문제에서 출발한다. 반대로 feed-forward reconstruction model은 dense geometry prior가 강하지만, online long sequence에서는 memory, latency, drift, scale consistency 문제가 남는다.

Problem: why inject a general 3D prior into dynamic SLAM?

The abstract frames the problem as robust pose estimation and 3D reconstruction in dynamic scenes. The proposed answer is a hybrid of patch-based BA and feed-forward reconstruction priors, where moving masks and depth predictions support online SLAM.

Abstract Core Claims

The system is not a simple concatenation of modules. The learned prior and BA correct each other's weaknesses.

ProblemPrior usedRole in SLAM
Moving objectsMotion probability from π3mosSamples static patches and reduces bad correspondences.
Monocular scaleDense depth predictionAligned with historical patch depths.
Depth uncertaintyPatch-depth covarianceControls frame-wise depth-prior weighting.

Context: why dynamic objects and monocular ambiguity are hard

The introduction contrasts two weaknesses. Classical VO/SLAM is online and geometrically consistent, but dynamic objects break data association. Feed-forward reconstruction models provide dense geometry priors, but batch-wise online use causes memory, latency, drift, and scale-consistency issues.

Problem / Proposal Structure

The paper links the online strength of SLAM with the prior strength of feed-forward reconstruction.

AxisLimitationConnection in this paper
Classical SLAMStatic assumption, sparse reconstruction, monocular ambiguity.Maintains online consistency through BA.
Feed-forward modelBatch inference, scale ambiguity, long-sequence drift.Injects only mask and depth priors into SLAM.
Hybrid resultEach side alone struggles with dynamic long sequences.Handles mask, scale, and uncertainty inside BA.

Gap: what is missing between SLAM and feed-forward 3D priors?

The related work separates dynamic SLAM methods that explicitly remove moving objects from feed-forward reconstruction methods that directly predict dense geometry and camera pose.

Why a 3D prior is needed

The motivation is that dynamic masks and feed-forward priors are both useful, but neither alone guarantees stable online SLAM.

Mechanism: how the 3D prior enters the SLAM objective

The method builds on DPV-SLAM and injects motion probability, dense depth, and confidence from π3mos into sliding-window BA. The target outputs are online camera poses Ti ∈ SE(3) and scale-consistent depth maps Di.

Evidence: which dynamic scenes test it?

The experiments cover moving object segmentation, camera tracking, video depth estimation, and ablations. The central message is that the mask improves dynamic filtering, while scale-aligned depth priors and uncertainty-aware BA improve tracking and depth consistency.

Usage / Limits: when prior-based dynamic SLAM is useful

The paper proposes a dynamic monocular visual SLAM system that brings a feed-forward 3D prior into SLAM optimization as moving masks, depth predictions, and uncertainty-aware depth priors. π3mos predicts motion probabilities and depths, then scale alignment and adaptive weighting stabilize online pose/depth estimation.
